Project Overview

Kid's gravitate to sports. Many of us reflect on the formative years and landmark our past with the memorable moments we shared. However, some kids do not possess the instinctual desire for reading articles and speaking those words outloud. As elder mentoring sports fans teach their children to read and speak at an elementary level, we want them to join the conversation we share as seasoned fans. In this way, bridging the uncanny valley between spanning the kid's disjointed sentences tonality, reading words by the syllables, and failing to enjoy the process can be daunting; ask any school teacher.

For this issue, I wanted to create an app without distractions, only sports and only reading. Everytime someone reads the article outloud, they get to see the highlights. We should incentivize the kids to see what they want to see. To activate their imaginations before seeing the spectacular. In this way, I hope they start to see the sport as we do.

Project Research

The space is saturated. Several sports apps. Several read along apps. Features for both sports and reading pop-up daily. Most of the largest conglomerates already compete in this area. The next generation of fans is this generation of kids.

An initial Stengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats analysis.

So, how do we stand out from the crowd? How are we different?

We stick to the basics. Only sports. Only reading. We put the kid's education and fun first and foremost. The philosophy of the app must be the driving force to compete. No ads. No distractions. We become the go-to website for younger kids. An alternative to the louder, ad-ridden, complex apps and websites.

App Design

We act as representatives of the league to kids directly. The imagery only approved by the Leagues themselves.

Messages only consistent with those values we want to bestow upon the young viewers. We create a small footprint. Allow the space for the reading. We build the associations between the actions and the terminology. In a way, sports retains a certain lexicon of meaningful phrase understood by experience alone.

Directed learning always outpaces the alternative.

Tasks For Users

The task is simple. Read the article. Watch the highlights. Repeat.

  1. Opening the app and Activating the Microphone for Speech Recognition.
  2. Reading the sports content. Interactive reading experience. Short Sports Clips as Rewards.
  3. Review the quality of reading. Personal User Stats

Once again, the simplicity of the app and it's direction is the allure. Read. Watch. Repeat.

Usability Testing & Further Development

To assess the usability of the app, fellow students and classmates were directed to the Figma prototype.

Early user test showed confusion in the navigation of the app via Figma. The screens intended to be splash loading screen were interpreted as articles lacking the readable content as other pages.

Thus, all entrances into the sports article rotation included articles.

For sport articles, content is king. The more content on the site the more chances a person has to engage and enjoy the experience.

Currently, the application is available for review on a Figma prototype as seen below.

Future development plans on implementation and deployment of the app. Accessing writer and articles for the young kids to read. Contacting the intellectual property owners of the materials needed to populate the website.

Summary

Read Along Sports is a mobile application designed to provide an interactive reading experience for young athletes. The app combines sports content with speech recognition technology to create an engaging learning environment. If there is any interest in contributing to this endeavour, contact my email below.

I think the most important learnt was the importance of directed brainstorming, structured analysis, and adapting feedback. While working along, these phases blend into each other and cause other problems down the road; like scope creep. Keeping the focus narrow and focused on the individual task or the user experience is the capstone for success.

I found the learning curve to build with Figma pretty steep. I wanted to include more animation and interactivity, yet the foramte limited these aspirations. Most rewarding was clicking through the prototype from page to page and into a loop. Accessing those processes opens up the mind to explore many other ideas and avenues.

Thank you to Figma for prototyping allowed for rapid iteration and user feedback.
